# Behavioral (STAR Method)

## What interviewers evaluate

- Ability to tell a specific, real story rather than a hypothetical or generic answer.
- STAR structure: Situation, Task, Action, Result.
- Ownership: what *you* did, using "I" rather than only "we".
- Measurable or concrete results, and honest reflection on what you learned.
- Values: teamwork, handling conflict, learning from failure, initiative.

## STAR method

- **Situation:** brief context (where, when, who). One or two sentences.
- **Task:** your responsibility or the challenge.
- **Action:** the specific steps *you* took. This should be the longest part.
- **Result:** the outcome, ideally measurable, plus what you learned.

## Question bank

### Junior

#### Tell me about a time you worked in a team and faced a disagreement.

**Strong answer includes:** a specific project (e.g. final-year project), the disagreement's
substance, how they listened, proposed a way to decide (data, a quick prototype, a deadline-based
compromise), the outcome, and what they would do differently.

**Common weak answers:** "We never had conflicts" or a vague "we talked and solved it."

**Red flags:** blames teammates entirely; no self-reflection.

#### Tell me about a time you failed or made a mistake.

**Strong answer includes:** a real mistake with real consequences, ownership without excuses, the
concrete fix, and a changed habit afterwards (e.g. now writes tests, asks earlier for help).

**Common weak answers:** a disguised strength ("I work too hard").

**Red flags:** cannot name any failure; blames others.

#### Describe a time you had to learn something new quickly.

**Strong answer includes:** the situation and deadline, how they learned (docs, small experiments,
asking people), what they built, and the result.

**Common weak answers:** "I watched YouTube videos" with no outcome.

**Red flags:** no evidence that the learning was applied.

### Mid

#### Tell me about a time you took initiative beyond your assigned work.

**Strong answer includes:** a problem they noticed, why it mattered, the action they took (with any
buy-in they got), measurable impact, and how they balanced it with assigned work.

**Common weak answers:** describes normal assigned work as initiative.

**Red flags:** acted unilaterally in a way that harmed the team.

#### Tell me about a time you had to deliver under a tight deadline.

**Strong answer includes:** how they prioritized (must-have vs nice-to-have), communicated tradeoffs
early, cut scope deliberately, and the result, including what quality risks they managed.

**Common weak answers:** "I worked all night."

**Red flags:** shipped something broken without telling anyone.

## Scoring anchors

Example question: "Tell me about a time you had a conflict with a teammate."

### Score 1/5

"I am easy to work with so I don't have conflicts." No story.

### Score 3/5

"In my FYP, a teammate wanted a different framework. We discussed it and chose mine." A story
exists but has thin actions, no reasoning, and a vague result.

### Score 5/5

"In my final-year project (S), I owned the backend and a teammate wanted to switch databases two
weeks before our demo (T). I asked for his concerns, then we spent an hour benchmarking both
options on our actual queries (A). The data showed our current setup met our needs, so we kept it
and logged his idea as future work. We demoed on time, and I learned to settle technical
disagreements with quick, shared experiments (R)." Specific, STAR-structured, ownership,
measurable outcome, reflection.
